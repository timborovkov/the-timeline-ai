What we're building

An evidence-derived operating record that turns everyday work into cited updates, digests, handoffs, answers, and object memory. Team members capture work as it happens — voice notes after a call, a forwarded email, a quick text dump at end of day, Slack messages, meeting transcripts, documents, and integration events — and The Timeline compiles it into a searchable event history of who did what, talked to whom, decided what, what changed, and why the system believes it.

The interaction model is voice-first and chat-first. People resist updating systems of record because those systems demand structure up front and then ask the team to report on work it already did. The Timeline accepts unstructured capture and derives the structure, so the cost of contributing is near zero.

The larger thesis: manually maintained systems of record are becoming obsolete for many teams. CRMs, project trackers, wikis, and status reports only know what people remembered to update. The actual source of truth is the event stream — the conversations, meetings, commits, documents, customer promises, decisions, and artifacts created while work happens. Timeline preserves that event history, extracts operational state from it, and keeps the important state approval-backed and cited.

Problem

Existing tools punish the act of recording. CRMs require you to know the contact, the deal stage, the next step before you can log anything. Wikis demand a page hierarchy. Ticket boards demand a project and status. The result: people don't update them, history is lost, and teammates ask each other "what happened with X" in Slack threads that themselves get lost.

The cost isn't just inconvenience — it's that organizational memory leaks. Conversations with clients, decisions made in meetings, the reasoning behind a choice three months ago — all of it lives in people's heads or scattered across tools.

The most visible symptom is duplicate communication work: stakeholder updates, daily digests, handoffs, project summaries, CRM hygiene, and "what happened with this account?" questions are reconstructed manually from work that already happened somewhere else.

Solution

One ingest pipeline, many capture surfaces. Voice notes, written notes, forwarded emails, Telegram and Slack messages, team documents, meetings, calendar rows, and third-party systems all flow into the same event stream. An LLM extracts useful facts ("Tim discussed SaaS licensing with John Ternus at Apple"), links them to existing objects when possible, and proposes approval-backed object changes only when the evidence is durable. Documents are processed into searchable chunks with version-aware citations. Calendar suggestions use the same approval-backed path: clear deadlines, meetings, recurring calls, proposed time slots, confirmations, and reschedules become reviewable calendar changes before they materialize. Everything important is embedded and indexed. An agent answers questions over the event history and knowledge base: what did the team work on yesterday, what changed on the Apple licensing deal, what's outstanding for Acme, what are our office rules, which projects have gone stale.

The product is intentionally low-structure on input and high-structure on output: the team does the work, Timeline generates the communication and memory from the evidence.

Target user

Small to mid-sized teams (5–50 people) doing knowledge work where context compounds: growth, consulting, product, implementation, customer success, and founder-led operations. The kind of team where one person being out of the loop on a client or project conversation creates real friction.

Core principles

Capture must be frictionless. If recording a thought takes more than five seconds of overhead, people won't do it. Voice memo via Telegram in three taps is the bar.
Raw events are immutable. Whatever was said or written goes in as-is and never changes. Derived facts can be re-extracted as models improve; the source of truth is the raw event.
Privacy is per-event, not per-team. A team member's end-of-day brain dump might include things they don't want broadcast verbatim. Visibility lives on the event.
The agent's answers are auditable. Every fact the agent surfaces links back to the raw event(s) it came from. No black-box summaries.
Re-processability is non-negotiable. Extraction prompts and models will improve. Replaying historical raw events through the new pipeline must be cheap and routine.

Architecture overview

Work surfaces and integrations

  • Web/PWA: typed notes, uploaded audio, drag-and-drop documents.
  • Telegram bot: voice memos, text, forwarded messages. Personal DMs and team group chats both supported. Plain messages land as timeline notes (DM captures are acknowledged with a 👀 reaction so capture is visible without adding group noise); /ask <question> runs the same agent as the web chat and replies in-thread, including for unlinked senders in bound group chats.
  • Slack bot: OAuth install, OAuth user linking, DMs, bound channels/private channels, /ask private answers, and @Timeline threaded answers. Bound-channel asks can answer for unlinked senders using team-visible history, and sender context is preserved even when the sender is not linked to a Timeline user.
  • Email ingest: forward, CC, or BCC to a per-team address. Postmark handles inbound parsing.
  • Team document library: curated folders, uploads, deletes, owners, timestamps, version history, source provenance, linked timeline events, and document changes. Event-backed attachments land in a captured-file inbox first, then become library documents only when promoted.
  • Meeting bots: the agent attends Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom calls (via Recall.ai or an equivalent provider), captures transcripts in real time, and writes decisions, action items, and timeline events to the same pipeline as everything else.
  • Work-system integrations: project updates, document changes, issues, PRs, reviews, commits, releases, CI activity, customer records, support queues, incidents, and operational systems can all become durable evidence through capture surfaces, first-party integrations, or ingest webhooks. GitHub, Linear, Google Drive, Monday.com, Slack workspace history, and Sentry are implemented native sync paths into the shared event pipeline. Teams can also connect arbitrary custom MCP servers — their own internal tools or any third-party MCP — so Timeline can reach the long-tail systems that matter to the team without implying passive ingestion by default.

Processing pipeline

  1. Raw event lands. Immutable record: source, author, team, timestamp, content reference (text inline, audio in RustFS).
  2. Transcription (if audio). OpenRouter /audio/transcriptions for short memos; larger stored audio is transcoded into provider-sized speech chunks before transcription.
  3. File processing (if file/document). Store immutable file versions in RustFS, distinguish unpromoted captured files from curated documents, extract source text and typed visual/transcript/metadata representations, and record timeline activity only for explicit document actions such as upload, promotion, or version changes.
  4. Extraction. LLM pulls useful facts and durable-work signals from the transcribed/raw/file representation text. Output is structured JSON linked back to the raw event or document version; noisy message mechanics stay raw evidence, while normalized shared links can become non-authoritative artifact-cluster evidence for timeline grouping. Extracted emails, phone numbers, and conservatively labeled addresses stay on raw-event metadata first; email and phone values graduate into person identity facets only through reviewable memory paths, while addresses remain location/object metadata unless explicitly promoted.
  5. Object resolution. Facts link to existing objects when names or aliases match a durable, non-generic workspace item. Generic topic phrases stay as fact text rather than becoming object memory by default, and everyday SaaS/tools should not become company objects just because the team mentioned them. Durable tool choices are modeled as decisions or updates to a specific existing artifact. New object memory goes through approval-backed proposals, and plausible duplicates are merged through human-reviewed cleanup.
  6. Embedding. Raw text, extracted facts, captured-file representations, document chunks, and document change summaries are embedded via OpenRouter (pinned to openai/text-embedding-3-small, 1536 dims unless changed through a re-embed procedure).
  7. Indexing. Vectors land in Qdrant with team-scoped payload filters; structured facts, objects, document metadata, and version history land in Postgres.

Query surfaces

  • Timeline view. Reverse-chronological feed, filterable by entity, author, date, source.
  • Entity pages. Auto-generated profile views: a client, a project, a person. Pulls every raw event and fact tagged to that entity.
  • Documents. Foldered team knowledge library with document owners, versions, previews, soft deletes, and timeline-linked change history, plus a Captured inbox where Telegram/Slack evidence can be reviewed and promoted.
  • Object boards. Curated Kanban/table/list work surfaces over durable objects: deals, companies, projects, people, tasks, documents, incidents, or team-defined object types. Boards keep explicit membership and board-local workflow state while object pages remain the canonical memory hub.
  • Calendar. Workspace-timezone-aware month/week/day views with ISO week numbers, all-day date-only events, and timeline-linked event audit rows.
  • Approvals. A single queue where users review agent and background suggestions, filter by pending/failed/resolved state, and accept or reject proposed tasks, object changes, and calendar changes before they become canonical state.
  • Agent chat. Natural-language queries over the timeline and document base. Tool-use pattern: agent queries Qdrant for semantic context, queries Postgres for structured filters and document versions, synthesizes an answer with event/document citations. Available with citation UI in the web app at /app/chat; Telegram and Slack bot asks share the same system prompt, tool set, and team scope, then deliver plain-text replies for chat readability.

Multi-tenancy model

  • team_id on every row, enforced at the query layer (not application logic).
  • Qdrant: one collection with mandatory team_id payload filter on every query. Reviewed if cross-team query patterns prove rare and stronger isolation guarantees become necessary — at which point move to per-team collections.
  • Row-level visibility on events (private / team / specific_users) layered on top of team isolation.

Data model (high level)

Five layers, deliberately separated:

  1. Raw events — append-only. Source, author, team, timestamp, content. Audio files in RustFS, text inline.
  2. Documents — team-owned files in folders, with immutable versions, owners, timestamps, visibility, source provenance, extracted text/chunks, and original blobs in RustFS.
  3. Extracted facts — LLM-derived, regenerable. Each fact links to the raw event(s) or document version(s) it came from and the existing objects it references.
  4. Objects/entities — Apple, John Ternus, "SaaS licensing deal Q2", "Office rules", "Website redesign", "Renew SOC2". Stable IDs, type, status/stage, owner, relationships, deduplication, human override. They are created intentionally or through approved durable-memory proposals, not from every raw mention.
  5. Object changes and tasks — structured status changes, field edits, tasks, completions, and agent-suggested updates. Manual changes are also timeline events.

A single raw event ("Met with John from Apple about licensing") produces multiple facts (Tim met John; topic was licensing; date was today), references multiple objects (Tim, John Ternus, Apple, the licensing deal), and may suggest object changes or tasks ("send revised deck by Friday"). All linked, all auditable.

Similarly, a manual document upload ("Office Rules.pdf") creates a curated document object, immutable version, timeline event, extracted chunks, embeddings, and citations. A conversational attachment starts as source-backed captured evidence instead; promoting it preserves provenance while making it manageable in the document drive. If Otto uploads a new version with a section banning TikTok at the office, the update is searchable as both document content and a timeline change.

Objects are broader than CRM records. A "deal" is one object type, but so is a project, person, vendor, hiring loop, document, bug, incident, or decision. The product should feel like object relation management: anything durable can have history, relationships, current state, tasks, and a timeline.

Telegram binding model

Hybrid: groups bound, DMs have an active team pointer.

  • Group chats are permanently bound to one team via /link <token>. The team's group chat is that team's. Bot must have privacy mode disabled to read non-command messages. Unlinked group members can ask against team-visible history without completing a personal link first.
  • DMs have a per-user active-team pointer. Users link multiple teams and switch via /team. /whereami confirms current attribution before sending sensitive content.
  • Link tokens are scoped (personal or group), 15-minute TTL, single-use. Group tokens require team-admin issuance.
  • Deep linking via t.me/YourBot?startgroup=TOKEN makes "add bot to group + bind" a single action.

AI provider strategy

Single inference layer through OpenRouter wherever possible:

  • Chat / summarization / extraction: OpenRouter via Vercel AI SDK. Model ids, context windows, capabilities, and chat-memory thresholds are code-owned in the shared model catalog, with Qwen / DeepSeek OSS models preferred for chat-style roles so runtime env cannot silently drift between services.
  • Embeddings: OpenRouter via Vercel AI SDK. Pinned to one model. Changing the embedding model invalidates the entire index — done only via documented re-embed procedure.
  • Transcription: OpenRouter speech-to-text via its JSON/base64 audio API. The model id is pinned in the same catalog; output is text and remains auditable on the raw event.
  • Fallback: direct provider access only for limitations OpenRouter hasn't yet covered.

Internal abstraction: llm.chatStructured(), llm.streamChat(), llm.embed(), llm.embedMany(), llm.transcribeAudio(), and llm.extractTextFromMedia() — keeps the rest of the codebase provider-agnostic and makes testing tractable.

Infrastructure

Local development

Docker Compose brings up the full stack: Postgres, Redis, Qdrant, RustFS (with auto bucket initialization). App and workers run on the host with hot reload via pnpm dev. A --profile app flag in compose runs everything containerized when prod parity matters.

Production / staging on Railway

Two Railway environments per project. Each runs:

  • web — Next.js app, public domain, Telegram + Postmark webhook receivers.
  • worker — BullMQ workers for transcription, extraction, embeddings, meetings, integrations, maintenance, and exports. Runs from apps/worker/railway.json.
  • Postgres — Railway managed plugin.
  • Redis — Railway managed plugin.
  • Qdrant — custom service from qdrant/qdrant image, persistent volume.
  • RustFS — custom service from rustfs/rustfs image, persistent volume.

Service-to-service references in Railway connect everything via private networking; no public endpoints for infra services. See docs/railway.html for the full deployment guide.

What's out of scope for v1

  • Full collaborative document editing. The internal drive stores and versions files; it does not replace Google Docs-style live editing.
  • Every bespoke integration on day one. The product starts with the work graph that matters most and expands first-party adapters as teams prove which systems are core to their daily workflow.
  • Real-time collaboration on entity pages. Later.
  • Mobile native apps. PWA covers this for v1.
  • End-to-end encryption. At-rest and in-transit encryption only; full E2EE conflicts with server-side LLM processing.

Expansion path

  • Knowledge base depth on RustFS: richer folder management, promotion workflows, and review surfaces for contracts, static docs, and captured evidence.
  • Document intelligence: deeper version diffs, cited policy answers, representation corrections, and targeted second-pass file inspection on top of the shipped PDF/image vision extraction and previews.
  • Object relation management: curated boards, object pages, task tracking, manual edits, board item histories, and "what changed" views for projects, people, companies, deals, vendors, incidents, documents, and team-defined object types.
  • More work-system integrations: expand first-party adapters across CRM, finance, project, support, engineering, and communication systems as they prove core to daily workflow. GitHub, Linear, Google Drive, Monday.com, Slack workspace history, and Sentry already use native sync into the shared event pipeline; MCP remains the reach layer for long-tail and experimental tools.
  • Meeting bots: join Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom calls (default provider Recall.ai), capture transcripts in real time, and write meeting events, notes, and extracted action items into the timeline like any other source.
  • External knowledge sync: map selected Google Drive folders/files into the internal document model. Start with changes, comments, metadata, titles, selected document summaries, and folder/project membership. Do not vectorize whole third-party repositories by default; embed deltas and curated summaries first, then full chunks for selected scopes.
  • Proactive agent: agent notices a forgotten follow-up and surfaces it. "You said you'd send Ternus a deck by Friday."
  • Team digests: personalized daily summaries per recipient, generated from visible activity and reused in email plus the Home Dashboard.
  • Export and portability: full team data export. Important for trust.

Risks and unknowns

  • RustFS maturity. Young storage layer holding customer voice memos and emails. Versioning enabled day one; nightly rclone sync to secondary store (Backblaze B2 or similar) from Railway cron; periodic restore drills.
  • Qdrant scaling. Vectors live in RAM. Monitor RAM usage from day one; plan to shard or move to managed Qdrant Cloud past a certain team count.
  • Entity resolution quality. "Apple" vs "Apple Inc" vs random Apple in a different context. Manual bulk merge now gives teams a human-in-the-loop cleanup path; AI-driven merge/archive suggestions now stay conservative and route merges through that same approval-backed merge preview rather than applying automatically. Merge reviews expose per-candidate related facts and can be rejected directly from the dialog.
  • LLM cost at scale. Re-extraction on prompt improvements is expensive. Cap and batch; consider cheaper extraction models for routine re-runs.
  • Telegram privacy mode trade-off. Disabling it means the bot sees every group message. Onboarding must be explicit about this — it's a feature for capture, but users need to know.
  • Vector index drift. Embedding model upgrades over time. Documented re-embed procedure from v1; never silent migration.
  • Railway lock-in. App services use Railpack via railway.json; local Compose and Dockerfiles remain available for portable builds. Migration cost to bare cloud is moderate but not zero.

Success criteria

  • A team member can record a voice note and have it queryable via the agent within 60 seconds of pressing send.
  • "What did we talk about with X last time" returns a correct, cited answer with >90% accuracy on populated teams.
  • "What are our office rules?" returns a cited answer from the right internal document version, including who updated it and when.
  • A team can inspect a project/deal/person/object and understand its current state, history, open tasks, and recent changes without manually maintaining a CRM.
  • A new team member can be onboarded to a team's timeline and become productive (asking the agent useful questions) within their first day.
  • Active teams contribute at least one event per active member per workday — capture friction is low enough that it becomes habitual.
