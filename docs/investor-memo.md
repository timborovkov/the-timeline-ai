# Timeline Investor Memo

## Thesis

The system of record for work is moving from manually maintained databases to
event-derived operational state.

Today, CRMs, project trackers, wikis, and status reports depend on humans
re-entering reality after work happens. That model breaks as work spreads across
Slack, meetings, docs, email, GitHub, Linear, Drive, calendar, and customer
conversations. The true source of truth is the event stream: what was said, what
changed, what was promised, who owned it, and which artifacts prove it.

Timeline captures that event history, preserves the evidence, extracts
structured state, and uses approvals to turn durable facts into operational
memory.

## Why Now

- LLMs can extract structure from messy work streams.
- Meeting transcripts, chat logs, docs, and integration APIs are increasingly
  available as digital event sources.
- Teams are overloaded by manual reporting, CRM hygiene, and context handoffs.
- AI answers without citations are not trusted for operational decisions.
- Smaller teams need the benefits of enterprise knowledge systems without
  enterprise process overhead.

## What Timeline Does

Timeline captures work as it happens and generates:

- Updates for stakeholders.
- Daily digests for what changed.
- Handoffs for people joining a project, client, or decision late.
- Cited answers to "what happened?" questions.
- Approval-backed project, account, task, object, and decision state.

The long-term product is an ambient system of record: operational memory derived
from evidence instead of manually maintained CRMs, trackers, and wikis.

## Wedge

Start with service-heavy and coordination-heavy teams where context loss is
expensive:

- Agencies and consultancies.
- Implementation and customer success teams.
- Founder-led operations teams.
- Product and engineering teams with dense Slack, meeting, doc, and project-tool
  history.

The initial value proposition is deliberately low-risk:

> Keep your current tools. Timeline turns them into cited project and client
> memory.

This lands as generated communication and expands into approved operational
state.

## Expansion Path

1. Capture work from existing tools.
2. Generate updates, digests, handoffs, and answers.
3. Build cited project and client memory.
4. Maintain approval-backed account/project/task/decision state.
5. Reduce or replace manual CRM and project tracker upkeep for teams that trust
   the evidence path.

## Differentiation

- Evidence first: every answer cites source events.
- Raw events are immutable; derived state can be regenerated.
- Approval-backed state prevents unchecked AI drift.
- Team and per-event visibility are core data-model concerns.
- The product starts beside existing tools and earns the right to become the
  operating record.

## Category Frame

Not simply enterprise search. Not simply a meeting notetaker. Not simply a wiki
or CRM.

Timeline is an event-derived operating record for teams.

## Key Risk

The biggest risk is not whether the product can be built; it is whether
customers understand the wedge quickly enough. The customer story must lead with
a painful workflow:

> Stop manually reporting on work your team already did.

The investor story can carry the larger transition:

> Manual systems of record are giving way to event-derived systems of record.
