# Timeline Demo Script

Use this for customer calls and quick product walkthroughs. Keep the demo anchored
to one recognizable workflow: a stakeholder asks what changed, and Timeline
answers from evidence.

## Setup

- Team: Acme Labs
- Project/account: Acme launch
- Sources visible in the demo: Slack, a meeting transcript, a document update,
  a task/project tool event, and the Timeline answer with citations.
- Buyer context: an operator, founder, delivery lead, or customer success leader
  who currently chases updates across people and tools.

## One-Sentence Open

Timeline captures work as it happens, then generates cited updates, digests,
handoffs, and answers so your team does not manually report on work it already
did.

## Demo Flow

1. Start with the painful question:

   > A stakeholder asks: "What changed with Acme, and what did we promise next?"

2. Show the source evidence already captured:

   - Slack thread: design approved onboarding copy.
   - Meeting transcript: Acme made SSO the launch blocker.
   - Document: launch checklist changed owner and rollout window.
   - Task/project event: migration task moved into implementation.

3. Ask Timeline the stakeholder question.

4. Show the generated answer:

   > Acme changed in three ways: SSO became the launch blocker, onboarding copy
   > was approved, and Priya now owns the migration checklist. The next promised
   > update is a confirmed SSO timeline by Friday.

5. Click each citation:

   - Open the meeting source for the SSO blocker.
   - Open the Slack source for onboarding copy approval.
   - Open the document version for the migration owner.

6. Show generated communication:

   - On-demand update for the stakeholder.
   - Daily digest entry that would have surfaced the same changes.
   - Handoff summary for a teammate joining the account.

7. Close with the adoption frame:

   > Your team keeps Slack, meetings, docs, tasks, and CRM. Timeline starts by
   > generating the updates and handoffs you already write manually. As trust
   > builds, the approved project and account memory becomes the operating
   > record.

## What Not To Demo First

- Do not lead with replacing HubSpot, Linear, Jira, or the wiki.
- Do not explain the full architecture before showing the stakeholder question.
- Do not call it generic "team memory" without tying it to updates, digests,
  handoffs, answers, and operational state.
- Do not hide the citations. The receipts are the product.

## Success Signal

The prospect says some version of:

> We already write this update manually.

or:

> This would have saved us from asking three people what happened.
